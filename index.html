MODULE 02 / EVIDENCE

The Reality Ledger

A 60-second view of an operational circuit breaker: preserve the conditions, check the evidence, and re-scope the action so work can proceed under review.

The Fluency Trap

A legacy tool’s visibly broken grammar announces its limits. A fluent model can resemble an overconfident associate: polished prose can hide missing records, dropped qualifications, or unsupported certainty.

Illustrative compression: “Tuesday or Wednesday” → “Tuesday, before the fall”. Day uncertainty hardens into an unsupported causal chronology. In the separate constructed system case, a 14-second approval appears fast while an OCR failure leaves litigation status unverified.

MODULE 03 / GOVERNANCE

How do organizations protect reviewers from signing unverified machine summaries—and account for the operational time real review requires?

Working manuscript

The Right to Hesitate:
AI, Art, and the Work of Judgment

The practical governance problem is the handoff from a fluent machine summary to a human decision. A useful circuit breaker makes missing evidence, dropped qualifications, and unresolved disputes visible before a reviewer signs.

The operational aim is auditable velocity: define what can proceed, what needs verification, and who owns the exception. A restricted informational memo can keep diligence moving while an unconditional approval remains blocked.

Read research abstract

Epistemic Hesitation as Operational Architecture: Deterministic Release Governance for High-Stakes Institutional AI

As frontier language models move from conversational assistants into institutional decision pipelines—across litigation, regulatory enforcement, and capital allocation—their primary failure mode shifts from obvious fabrication to fluent epistemic drift. Drawing on my working manuscript, The Right to Hesitate, this project argues that institutional safety cannot rely on post-hoc policy principles or single-metric leaderboards alone. Because probabilistic models flatten speaker doubt, contested evidentiary claims, and scope qualifications into confident prose, high-consequence workflows require formal architectural hesitation gates where machine fluency yields to accountable human judgment before irreversible downstream action occurs.

To operationalize that theory, I built the Reality Ledger reference architecture, translating governance standards including the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, and ISO 42001 into schema-bound retrieval handoffs, cryptographic human adjudication bindings, and five non-compensatory release circuit breakers. In an exploratory civil-opinion evaluation pilot, the methodology surfaced subtle attribution, pronoun, and scope drift in 8/10 summaries while preserving 4/4 speaker-doubt and cross-record dispute instances with 0/5 unsupported relational inferences—informing two-stage prompt and checklist workflows that reduced attorney verification rework by approximately 35% under FRCP Rule 11 guardrails. Validated across 166 automated tests, 3,378 routing parity checks, and 8/8 critical fault detections on a 16-case synthetic benchmark, this work establishes a portable standard for auditable human-AI oversight.

The working manuscript develops the theory; the Reality Ledger showcase and technical method (PDF) provide the companion implementation and evaluation context. This is a working manuscript, not a peer-reviewed publication claim.

The operational time cost of review

The Waiting Room

A circuit breaker must make room for real review. Verification needs time, an accountable reviewer, and a clear next action.

Verification-Adjusted Velocity

Start with gross AI drafting time saved, then subtract the mandatory human time needed for source checking, exception routing, and rework.

Time budget: gross AI drafting time saved − (mandatory human source-checking time + exception-routing time + rework time). This is an operating definition, not a measured pilot metric or a method prescribed by the references below.

If mandatory review costs exceed the value of the gross drafting time saved, net ROI can be negative. A restricted informational diligence memo can preserve verified speed by releasing supported information while unresolved claims remain blocked. The negative-ROI case is conditional; no measured ROI result is claimed.

  • Record what needs checking and who owns the decision.
  • Account for verification time, queue delay, and escalation.
  • Specify what restricted action can proceed while an exception remains open.
